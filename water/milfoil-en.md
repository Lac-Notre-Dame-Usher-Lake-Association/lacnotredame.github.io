[Milfoil link 1] What is Eurasian watermilfoil and why is it bad?
 
Eurasian watermilfoil, or milfoil for short,is an invasive aquatic plant that generally grows at depths of one to four meters but can take root in depths up to ten meters. It can form dense carpets on the lake’s surface interfering with swimming, fishing, boating and other activities. As the plant dies and decays at the end of each season it consumes oxygen dissolved in the water. This can have negative environmental consequences.
Milfoil reproduces through the fragmentation of its stems. This occurs naturally from mid-July to October, but it can also be drastically worsened through nautical activities and watercraft of all kinds – both motorized and non-motorized – are known to contribute to the creation and spread of milfoil fragments.

Milfoil outcompetes native plants for nutrients and over time can overtake most if not all natural aquatic vegetation.

Eurasian watermilfoil is estimated to have arrived in Lac Notre-Dame in 1994. Most likely the plant was first deposited in the lake by a boat or trailer carrying a small fragment of the weed. The first attempt to map the plant in 2004 found a small number of isolated beds in shallow areas of the lake. Ten years later in 2014, the freshwater consulting group ABV7 found the hearty milfoil plant had spread to more than 25% of the lake. Read the AVB7 report here. [LINK] 
Despite control efforts milfoil can now be found in most shallow areas of the lake.
Eurasian watermilfoil does not die when it is cut or pulled from the lake bottom. Instead, cuttings left in the water often grow into new plants. 
The amount of milfoil in the water can vary from year to year, depending on the weather and other factors that affect the plant’s growth.
The Lake Association was founded in 2004 in response to the Eurasian watermilfoil explosion in the lakes. The Association received valued advice and assistance from biologist Paul Hamilton of the Canadian Museum of Nature. Mr. Hamilton continues to support the Association’s efforts by analyzing water test results and advising us about important changes in the water quality.
An electric cutter was purchased before 2008 by the Association, with a financial contribution of $6,400 from the Municipality of La Peche. While the cutter was put to use initially it was soon found to be impractical. 
It is unlikely that the use of any cutter, electric or manual, would be authorized by the province.

[Milfoil link 2] Are herbicides the solution? The answer is NO

ProcellaCOR for milfoil is a permit-only herbicide!
Some property owners have asked whether ProcellaCOR can be used to control Eurasian milfoil. The answer is no — not without government permits, and possibly not at all.
Families draw drinking water from our lake
Health Canada requires permits from federal and provincial agencies before anyone can apply this product to a lake. The product label also says it cannot be used in water for irrigation. Families with young children draw their drinking water directly from our lake. Health Canada has determined permits are required to protect our families, fish and ecosystem.
Resistance problem
The manufacturer warns that weeds can become resistant to ProcellaCOR if it’s used repeatedly in the same area. After 25+ years of milfoil on our lake, using a product that could make the problem worse requires careful thought before applying for permits.
Individual property owners cannot legally apply this product
The lake association has not applied for permits and is not pursuing this option at this time.

[Milfoil link 3] Please follow Watercraft Guidelines to slow the spread of milfoil
Invasive Eurasian watermilfoil is well-established in Lac Notre-Dame. 
Today, milfoil is present throughout much of the lake because this hearty plant spreads easily and thrives in the many shallow areas of Lac Notre-Dame. A survey of residents in June 2025 found a high level of concern about milfoil’s impact on laker users and the environment. [link to the survey report]
In July 2025, members attending the Annual meeting of the Association unanimously adopted Watercraft Guidelines to slow the spread of the weed.

[Milfoil link 5] Naturalize your shoreline 
Clearing vegetation on your waterfront lot to the shoreline provides a clear pathway into the lake for fertilizer, sediment and other “food” milfoil thrives on. 
You can stop feeding the weeds in this manner by maintaining the natural vegetation along the shoreline or restoring shorelines to their natural condition.  If your shoreline lacks a natural barrier that filters weed feeding nutrients before they get into the water, please restore your shoreline by planting native species like Red Osier Dogwood, Pussy Willow, Sweet Gale and Blue Flag iris. See the Ottawa River Keeper Guide for more information.

[Milfoil link 6] Provincial Rules and Regulations 
The Government of Quebec has established strict rules and requirements for anyone who takes steps to manage the presence of Eurasian watermilfoil in a waterbody in Quebec. These rules and requirements are intended to protect native plant and animal species in and around our lakes.
The Lake Association’s duty is to inform you of these legal requirements.
As individuals or as an Association, we are not allowed to cut milfoil with a manual or electric cutter or with a harvester without a formal authorization from the Quebec government, nor can we install burlap or another cover on the lake bottom covering an area larger than 75m2, no matter the size of the waterbody.
A second authorization may be required related to the conservation and development of wildlife.
The process of obtaining provincial authorizations is both onerous and expensive and would require broad support from lake residents.
The authorization process is explained here [link https://www.environnement.gouv.qc.ca/autorisations/autorisations-ministerielles.htm#nouvelle-demande]
Members have mandated the Lake Association to develop a framework to deal with the milfoil infestation on our lake. The Lake Association Board is considering all legal options for our milfoil control program for 2026. If you would like to get involved in this effort, please volunteer at info@lacnotredame.org.
